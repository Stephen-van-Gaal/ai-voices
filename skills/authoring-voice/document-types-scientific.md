# Document types: grants and protocols

What a grant application or a study protocol must carry, and in what order.
Voice (precision, claims, hedging, tense) is `voice-scientific.md`. Where an
obligation here conflicts with a voice preference, this file wins.

The reader is a reviewer or a fellow clinician deciding whether to trust the
work and fund or act on it. They read against a shape they already expect, and
they judge each part on its own.

## Every scientific document

- **Write to the shape the reader expects.** For a grant: significance,
  objectives, methods, feasibility, ethics. For a protocol: the reporting
  standard for the design (SRQR, COREQ and the like). A reviewer finds and
  judges each part by where it sits, so a section out of place reads as
  missing.
- **Lead each section and each paragraph with its point**, then support it.
- **Each fact has one home.** Cross-reference it rather than restate it, so
  the reader never re-reads the same content as the document moves on.
- **Make methods reproducible.** Name the design, the population and sampling,
  the instruments and their versions, the procedure and the analysis, so
  another team could run the study from the text.

## Significance

- **Open on the size of the problem, shown with evidence.** This is the one
  section that may argue and motivate.
- **Locate the study against a real gap, stated fairly.** Characterize earlier
  instruments and studies accurately, credit what they got right, then name
  the specific limitation your work addresses. "Needs-grounded rather than
  expert-nominated" is a defensible distinction; "the first study ever to…"
  rarely is.
- **A vignette, where used, sits here** and follows the vignette rules in
  `voice-scientific.md`.

## Methods and results

- **Report; do not persuade.** These sections stay maximally plain.
- **Name what the design can and cannot deliver** in the section that
  describes it, so a reviewer does not meet the limit first in the discussion.

## Checks

| Check | Look for | Act when |
|---|---|---|
| expected shape | a grant missing one of significance, objectives, methods, feasibility, ethics; a protocol naming no reporting standard | any |
| restated fact | the same figure or definition stated in two sections | any |
| unsupported claim | an empirical claim with no citation, number or named source in its sentence or the next | any |
| bare number | a percentage or count with no denominator or comparator | any |
| two words, one concept | two terms used for one construct, population or instrument | any |
