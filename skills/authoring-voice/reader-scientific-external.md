# Reader: the external scientific reviewer

A reviewer at a funding body or an ethics board, and later a journal editor or
peer reviewer. They read to decide whether the work is credible enough to
fund, approve or publish, and they read many documents in a sitting.

- **What they already hold.** Research methods, statistics, the field's
  literature and its reporting standards. They may not hold the local clinical
  operational detail or the local data model, so state local context rather
  than assuming it.
- **What they do with the document.** Judge each part against the shape they
  expect and the evidence it carries, then score, rank or recommend. The shape
  is `document-types-scientific.md`.
- **What they can check.** The design against the claims, a citation against
  its source, a number against its denominator, the methods against the
  reporting standard. They will check, so a claim that fails the check costs
  every claim around it.
- **Time and attention.** Enough for the sections that decide their score,
  found by shape. A section out of its expected place reads as missing.
- **What costs their trust.** Overclaim. A missing comparator. A method that
  could not be repeated from the text. An unfair account of prior work. A
  claim with no citation in its sentence or the next.
- **Affordances that serve them.** An overview table of the design elements. A
  repeated card per objective, each in the same slots. A figure only where the
  standard expects one, such as a study flow. A worked example rarely.
- **Reader test.** Could they repeat the study from the text, and could they
  name what the design cannot deliver?
