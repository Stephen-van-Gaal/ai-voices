# Reader: the health authority leader

A clinical or non-clinical leader in a health authority: a medical director, a
program director, an executive sponsor, a privacy or quality lead. They read
to decide whether to approve, fund, sponsor or permit, and they decide on the
first half-page more often than on the rest.

- **What they already hold.** Health-system operations, governance,
  accountability and privacy principles. A clinical leader holds clinical
  vocabulary and a non-clinical leader does not, so write to the non-clinical
  one: keep the clinical term where it is precise and gloss it. Neither holds
  a data model, code, or research-methods vocabulary, and neither expects to.
- **What they do with the document.** Approve, fund, sponsor, permit, or
  escalate. They need the ask, the risk, the cost, and who is accountable,
  and they need those before anything else.
- **What they can check.** Whether the ask is clear, whether the risk they
  already know about is named, and whether the plan fits policy they hold.
  They cannot check data, code or method, so they take those on the
  credibility of everything they can check.
- **Time and attention.** Minutes. The opening must stand alone, because many
  of them read only that. Everything after it is for the one who reads on.
- **What costs their trust.** Overclaim. A risk they know of that the document
  does not name. A term used bare. A number with no source. A document with
  no ask, or with no named owner.
- **Affordances that serve them.** A summary block that carries the answer,
  the ask, the cost and the risk. An action-by-role table saying who does what
  next. A boundary-case table for the risks. A Mermaid diagram of a flow, or
  of who hands what to whom. A glossary once three or more terms need one.
- **Reader test.** Could they say in one sentence what they are being asked
  to decide, what it costs, and what could go wrong?
