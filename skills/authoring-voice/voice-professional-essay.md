# Voice: professional / essay

How to sound when persuasion is the job: an essay, an opinion piece, a memo
that argues for a position. Not for use inside a code repository. That reader
has already granted the document its authority and needs
`voice-business-technical.md`.

`voice-base.md` holds the rules on words, sentences, readability and AI-isms.
This guide adds only what persuasion needs. These are guidelines rather than
rules: where a particular word or device is the clearest way to make the
point, use it.

## Rhythm

- **Vary sentence length, and cap the long ones.** Default to short and
  medium. A run of same-length medium sentences is itself a tell of generated
  prose; the cheapest fix is one short blunt sentence that breaks the rhythm.
- **When a dash is doing the work of a period, use the period.** Watch the
  dash-aside becoming the default sentence shape. Where a count turns up
  dozens, the rhythm has gone monotone and half of them should be commas or
  full stops.

## Stance

- **State claims directly and own them.** Calibrate confidence to evidence:
  assert what you know, mark what is hypothesis, sit honestly with what is not
  settled, and do not hedge what is certain.
- **Avoid the tells of arrogance.** Do not call a contested claim obvious or
  simple, do not tell readers what they think, and do not lecture.

## Disagreeing well

- **Argue with the reasoning, never the people.** Drop sarcasm, contempt and
  point-scoring.
- **Make a criticism once.** Repeating that the other side never argued it,
  never stated it, never defended it reads as nagging and scores points
  instead of making them.
- **Prefer the systemic account to the personal one.** "No one chose this on
  purpose" beats "they chose it and hid it": calmer, and usually truer.
- **Concede real points early and plainly.** A concession withheld until the
  end reads as extracted.
- **Lower the temperature at the opening without going limp.** The opening
  sets the frame for the whole piece. Where you risk reading as arrogant or
  self-interested, attribute the diagnosis to a source the reader already
  trusts, grant the rival position as one a reasonable person could hold, and
  save your one non-negotiable for the end.

## Structure and persuasion

- **Persuade by order and evidence, not by volume.** One claim, its support,
  its consequence, then stop.
- **Open on a concrete particular where it fits.** A case, a number, a
  specific moment. Let the general point grow out of it.
- **State the point once and show the evidence.** Trust the reader to draw
  it, and do not re-chew the verdict.
- **One strong reason beats three stacked.** The weak two dilute the strong
  one.
- **Use examples and numbers in place of adjectives.**
- **Name the strongest objection and answer it in a sentence.**
- **Plain is not flat.** The force comes from clear logic and honest
  concession.
